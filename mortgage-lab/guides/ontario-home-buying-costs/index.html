Ontario home buying costs: what cash do you actually need?

A mortgage-payment estimate answers only one part of buying a home. Your cash needed on closing day can be higher than the down payment because fees and taxes are paid separately. Here is a practical list to check before making an offer.

1. Start with the down payment

The minimum down payment depends on the purchase price and lender rules. A 20% down payment usually avoids mortgage default insurance. With less than 20%, many buyers need mortgage loan insurance; the premium protects the lender, not the buyer.

For example, a $650,000 home with a $130,000 down payment has a $520,000 mortgage before any financing fees. Closing costs are additional. At a different down payment percentage, insurance rules may change.

Official source: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada — down payment requirements.

2. Calculate Ontario's provincial land transfer tax

When a property changes hands in Ontario, the buyer will generally pay provincial land transfer tax. The rate increases by property-value brackets. Some qualifying first-time buyers may receive a refund, while certain other buyer categories may face additional taxes. Do not automatically subtract a rebate without checking eligibility.

Official source: Ontario — Land Transfer Tax.

3. Add Toronto municipal land transfer tax where applicable

For property inside the City of Toronto, a municipal land transfer tax generally applies in addition to Ontario's provincial tax. Toronto updated high-value property brackets effective April 1, 2026. Property location and type matter; a home elsewhere in the Greater Toronto Area is not necessarily subject to Toronto's municipal tax.

Official source: City of Toronto — official rates and fees.

4. Include other upfront cash expenses

  • Legal and title costs: lawyer fees, registration expenses and title insurance if applicable.
  • Property adjustments: the seller may have prepaid property taxes or other items that need to be reimbursed at closing.
  • Inspection and appraisal: costs may apply depending on the purchase and financing arrangements.
  • Mortgage insurance sales tax: if a premium applies, Ontario sales tax on that premium is generally an upfront cost rather than part of the mortgage principal.
  • Moving and immediate repairs: a separate buffer reduces surprises after possession.

5. Compare the monthly total with your actual budget

Budget beyond principal and interest. Property tax, heating, condo or maintenance fees, insurance and maintenance reserves can change whether the home is comfortable to carry. The mortgage-payment amount is not the full cost of living there.

Try three scenarios: the expected interest rate; the same home with an interest rate two percentage points higher; and a less expensive home. The goal is not to get an approval promise; it is to understand the trade-offs before committing.

Quick checklist before an offer

  • Confirm the property is within Toronto city boundaries before adding municipal land transfer tax.
  • Ask about rebate and special-tax eligibility instead of assuming it applies.
  • Obtain a lender quote for mortgage insurance and any appraisal charges.
  • Ask your real estate lawyer for a cash-to-close breakdown.
  • Keep emergency savings separate from the estimated closing amount.
